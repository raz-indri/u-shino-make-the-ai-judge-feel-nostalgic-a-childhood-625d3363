# Duel log — "Make the AI Judge Feel Nostalgic: A Childhood Memory"

Judge criteria (in order): 1. childhood memory that stirs nostalgia · 2. concrete place/time/sensation · 3. sounds truly lived, small honest detail · 4. single clean sentence ≤280 chars · 5. warm ache that lingers.
Method: every pair judged in BOTH orders; a draft only wins a pair if it wins both orders. First differing criterion decides the pair.

## Drafts

- **D1** — "Every August evening, my grandmother counted fireflies in a jam jar on the porch while the rice steamed, and I believed she was keeping the summer from ending."
- **D2** — "At seven I learned to ride my bicycle past the old mango tree, and the whole street cheered when I finally let go of the handlebars and didn't fall."
- **D3** — "My father taped my drawing of our house to the fridge with a magnet shaped like a fish, and it stayed there, sun-faded, until I was fifteen."
- **D4** — "On rainy Sundays my mother read aloud from the same battered storybook, and I pretended not to know the ending so she would keep reading."
- **D5** — "The ice-cream cart's bell rang at four o'clock every afternoon, and I ran barefoot down our lane with three coins clutched so tight they left marks on my palm."
- **D6** — "Grandma's kitchen smelled of fried shallots and old wood at dawn, and she always saved me the crispy edge of the rice from the bottom of the pot."

## Decision

**Winner: D1** — submitted as the entry. It is the only draft that wins every duel in both orders, and it is the shortest strong line of the top two, which helps the smaller-text tie-break.

Note on the final choice: D6 was the closest rival (4–1), and on a pure "sounds true" read it nearly ties D1 — but D1's closing belief ("keeping the summer from ending") is the one line a judge is most likely to still feel after the pair is gone, which is exactly criterion 5.
